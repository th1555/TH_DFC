How leagues compare

Scoring in the Highland League is easier than scoring in the Championship. So each season's goals and assists are scaled by that league's rate before being compared, with Scottish League Two fixed at 1.00.

Below 1 means a weaker league, so the numbers are scaled down; above 1 means stronger, scaled up. The rates are learned from players who actually moved between leagues and how their scoring changed. Shaded rows rest on only a few moves and are rougher.

The adjusted figure: for each season, goals plus assists multiplied by that league's rate; added up, then divided by the games played. Seasons in a league without a rate are left out of it.

What this shows, and what it doesn't

It shows

  • Recorded games, goals and assists for every player, season by season.
  • A fair comparison across leagues of different strength.
  • Far more players than anyone has time to watch, filterable in seconds.

It doesn't show

  • Style, movement, defensive work, attitude or fitness. That still needs someone to watch him.
  • The difference between two players with the same numbers. That needs paid match data such as Wyscout.
  • Contracts or wages.
